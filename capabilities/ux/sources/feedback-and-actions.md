# Reason about feedback and action continuity

Use the shared decision process in `workflow` through the [UX reference index](../../../references/index.json). This source owns the information a person needs after an action, its scope and lifetime. It does not map events to notifications or prescribe their location, duration or focus target.

## Identify the information gap

Establish what the person intended, what they can already perceive from the resulting interface, and what remains uncertain or requires a next action. Consider consequence, reversibility, delay, repetition and the need to revisit a result. A completed event and a continuing condition have different communication needs.

Ask what the person would misunderstand, miss or be unable to do without additional feedback. Compare that with what the changed content, control state and task context already communicate. Decide whether a gap exists before choosing a message. Repeating a visible result may add attention cost without adding information. Conversely, a result that is delayed, out of context or unavailable through the person's access mode may need a separate signal. These are considerations for the model, not an event-to-treatment table.

## Choose a sufficient treatment

Choose what information to communicate, to whom, when it is useful, and how it supports the next action. Distinguish the needs served by visible content, an assistive announcement and a focus move; a need for one does not automatically establish a need for all. Preserve applicable product and accessibility contracts while avoiding redundant channels chosen merely because they are available. Evaluate each channel in the access mode it serves: visible change, semantic state, focus and an announcement are not interchangeable evidence of what every user perceives. A returned focus or exposed state may already convey part of the result; an added message needs an identified remaining contribution. Do not translate every implementation event into a visible confirmation or an assistive announcement. Resolve an asserted requirement through `selection` and the applicable original guidance rather than assuming that more feedback is inherently more accessible.

Updated content, an announcement, a temporary notice and a durable result surface are possible tools. The model decides whether an additional treatment is needed and which form fits. Place the information in a relationship the person can understand: what does it refer to, when must it be found again, and when does it cease to be relevant? A justified information need does not settle how to introduce it. Follow `interactions` for the effect of its appearance and disappearance on the continuing task, including correction and retry; follow `visual-expression` for the combined signals and prominence. Use those owners before accepting the treatment rather than treating message delivery as completion.

## Revisit the information decision in use

Follow the affected task and assess whether the person can identify the current outcome and next action, retain or recover their work, and find information that remains necessary later. Observe replacement, disappearance and context changes when they matter. Inspect the relationship between explanation, optional detail and the next action as a composition, including their relative emphasis and proximity. When that relationship is unsettled, use `selection` for a relevant concrete comparison rather than treating the presence of each part as sufficient. Keep screen visibility, accessibility-tree exposure and observed assistive output separate; evidence for one does not prove the others. Reconsider the need for the treatment as well as its implementation: an accurately displayed message may still duplicate information or draw attention away from the task. An omitted message is suitable only when necessary understanding and access remain intact.

Use the consuming project's selected verification source for evidence and completion. Relevant external references through the same UX index include `primer-feedback`, `carbon-notification`, `gov-banner`, `sap-partial-edit` and `carbon-forms`. Read a source when its scope helps the current decision; do not transplant its product-specific treatment or fetch the catalogue exhaustively.
